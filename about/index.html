About

I'm a PhD scholar in the Department of Computer Science and Information Systems at BITS Pilani, KK Birla Goa Campus, working at the intersection of hardware security, RISC-V architectures, and low-level software. My current work spans microarchitectural side-channel attacks and their mitigation, memory encryption, formal verification of cache coherency, and the security of CXL memory fabrics. I have six patents filed in the RISC-V space covering data-flow integrity, crypto engines, and on-chip ML, and earlier I designed Ku/X-band antennas at KIIT.

Outside of research I spend a lot of time building things close to the metal: a TCP/IP stack from scratch, a malloc replacement, a cycle-accurate 6502 emulator, a container runtime using raw namespaces and seccomp, a bytecode VM in Go, and neural networks built purely from matrix operations. This is not an exhaustive list — I keep adding projects to my GitHub profile as a hobby. I also write about these topics on the blog, and I teach sometimes.

A C program is nothing but bytes in memory — there is no abstraction beyond that you have to take on faith. More than low levels per se, I am interested in the stripped-down, non-abstracted, naked-truth stuff.
